# Source priority

Choose sources by their ability to establish the specific claim, not by domain reputation alone. `Primary`, `authoritative`, and `reliable` are related but not interchangeable.

## Default order

1. **Direct primary evidence:** original records, datasets, legal text, standards, source code or release artifacts, contemporaneous documents, first-hand interviews, original papers, filings, product documentation, and official policy statements.
2. **Authoritative synthesis:** public-agency guidance, standards commentary, systematic reviews, scholarly reference works, or institutional summaries that interpret a body of primary evidence.
3. **High-quality secondary analysis:** peer-reviewed commentary, established journalism, specialist publications, books, and expert analysis with visible sourcing.
4. **Discovery-only material:** aggregators, unsourced summaries, forums, social posts, AI-generated pages, and search snippets. Use these to locate stronger evidence, not as the default basis for a claim.

The order is a search priority, not an automatic credibility score. Evaluate authenticity, relevance, independence, currency, methodology, and conflicts of interest for every source.

## Domain guidance

### SEO and platform behavior

Prefer official search-engine documentation, status pages, developer documentation, patents only for what they disclose, named employee statements with context, and observable product interfaces. Use SEO publications for experiments, interpretation, and discovery. Do not convert an industry consensus or correlation study into an official ranking rule.

### Public policy, law, and government

Prefer enacted text, regulations, court decisions, legislative records, official statistics, agency publications, procurement or budget records, and the responsible authority's current pages. Confirm jurisdiction, effective date, amendments, and whether guidance is binding. News coverage is context, not the legal rule.

### Research and technical claims

Prefer the original paper, registered report, dataset, protocol, standards document, repository, or technical specification. Record study design, sample, version, limitations, correction or retraction status, and whether a preprint has been peer reviewed. Reviews and meta-analyses are authoritative syntheses, not primary evidence for each included observation.

### Products, services, and companies

Prefer current official documentation, pricing and policy pages, release notes, security advisories, filings, status pages, and direct demonstrations. Treat vendor claims as primary evidence of what the vendor says, not independent proof of quality, performance, adoption, or safety. Use independent testing or reporting for those claims.

### Folklore, occult topics, and historical traditions

Prefer dated newspapers, archival records, field notes, oral-history collections, contemporaneous publications, museum or library collections, scholarly editions, and research that identifies its sources. Distinguish the earliest located attestation from the origin of a tradition. A later retelling is primary evidence for that retelling, not automatically for an earlier event.

### General current facts

Prefer the entity responsible for the fact, then independent authoritative confirmation when the claim is consequential or self-serving. For changing facts, verify the page's update date, the data period, and current applicability.

## Evaluation record

For each retained source, record:

- source class: `primary`, `authoritative synthesis`, or `secondary`;
- authority for this claim: who produced it and why they can know;
- independence and incentives;
- currency and applicable period;
- directness: whether it reports the observation or repeats another source;
- limitations: access, methodology, missing context, version, jurisdiction, or potential bias.

Multiple articles repeating one press release count as one evidence chain. Trace citations upstream and record the earliest inspectable source.

## When primary evidence is absent

Do not promote a secondary source to primary. State `No suitable primary source located`, list the repositories, organizations, query families, languages, date ranges, or archives checked at a useful level, and distinguish `not found` from `does not exist`. Use the best secondary evidence only with its limitations and an appropriately lower claim status or confidence.
